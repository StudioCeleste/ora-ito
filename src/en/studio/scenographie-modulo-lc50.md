<strong>LC50 by Ora ïto</strong>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Ora ïto’s project for Cassina, on the occasion of Designer’s Days, is part of the LC50 program in homage to Le Corbusier. This project features two essential symbols of the architect's work: the Open Hand and the Modulor. Made of wood, the material which constitutes the Roquebrune-Cap-Martin shed, these symbols alone express the work of the Master.</p>
<strong>The Modulor</strong>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Born from the meeting of the Module and the Golden Ratio, the Modulor is an architectural concept invented by Le Corbusier in 1944. A standardized human silhouette used to design the structure and size of the housing units designed by the architect, such as the Cité radieuse of Marseille, the Maison radieuse of Rezé or the Unité d'habitation of Firminy-Vert. According to him, it should allow maximum comfort in the relationships between Man and his living space. Measuring 1.83m tall, the figure reaches a height of 2.26m with his arm raised.</p>
<strong>The Open Hand</strong>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Designed <i>“to receive and to give”</i>. The open hand is above all the symbol of exchange and the passing of knowledge. The symbolic expression of the message that Le Corbusier wanted to express in all of his work. The philosophy of the architect, the painter and the poet that he was, finds here an eminently fraternal sign which passes the time and speaks to all.</p>
<p class="feat-p"><i>“Recognize this open hand, the open hand raised as a sign of reconciliation – open to receive, open to give”</i></p>
<strong>The scenography</strong>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The scenography imagined by Ora ïto plays on the relationship of scale. Playing with the codes of Le Corbusier, he features furniture designed by the architect, Pierre Jeanneret, Charlotte Perriand (LC1, LC3, LC4, LC14) inside wooden architectural elements, thus referring to the Cabanon, which themselves, when grouped together, signify a city. <i>“All Le Corbusier is there” </i>at least the symbols of his work, and presents other signs such as the course of the sun. The wall fresco represents the Modulor, from the sitting position to the standing position, and expresses the master's desire to propose, thus, a system more adapted than the metric system, because it is directly linked to human morphology, the replacement of which he hoped to one day see. <i>“Le Corbusier is one of the people who made me want to do my job”</i>, Ora Ito likes to say <i>“and it is thanks to Cassina if I discovered this great theoretician and complete artist”</i>. In his youth, he discovered a catalog of the Italian brand and stopped in front of the Maestri furniture.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Today, Ora Ito continues to admire Le Corbusier and to bring him to life. Ora Ito collects the hands drawn by the architect, and has a centerpiece of his architecture with the former gymnasium on the roof terrace of the Cité radieuse which he transformed into an art center: the MAMO. Thus, through the exhibitions that Ora Ito programs, <i>“Le Cobusier’s work is everywhere! and we contribute to his memory »</i>. The artists Xavier Veilhan, Daniel Buren and Dan Graham have already set up shop there.</p>
