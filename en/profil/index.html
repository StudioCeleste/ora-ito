profile

Simplicity is the art of making complicated things simple.

This therefore consists of developing a simple answer to a complex problem. To be simple without being simplistic, the job of a designer consists of finding the solution integrating the maximum complexity in an envelope that is as simple as possible, of bringing together the maximum constraints behind the facade that is most obvious to understand and use.

A simplex shape is a shape that appears simple but incorporates invisible complexity. The objective is to provide the user of the object with a relationship devoid of any complications. The simpler the object is to use, the more intuitive, easy and natural the user's approach becomes. A “simplex” object therefore has a simple appearance but which intelligently integrates its functionality.

“Simplex” is a neologism that combines two words: Simple and Complex.

From a plastic and industrial point of view, this involves erasing the rough edges of an object as much as possible to make it as accessible as possible. No one needs to add complexity to their daily lives, but everyone needs to find the best solutions to simplify their lives. Simplexity is therefore a philosophical bias which has a concrete application: making simpler what is too complicated.

The simple appearance of an object is very complex to achieve. I could also draw a parallel with a classical dancer who ideally illustrates what simplexity is... We must not see the work which supports the purity of the movement, we must not feel the effort which is hidden behind the beauty of a gesture which must for the spectator seem as simple as possible. Simplicity will consist of the dancer erasing the complexity of her work to offer the public a smooth, perfectly controlled appearance, without ever suggesting the constraints that this implies.

For an object, it's the same thing, it must serve us in a very complete way and must not let us know it. We don't want an object to show us what is hidden behind its design and manufacturing.

My job consists of offering the consumer an object devoid of any complications which gives the appearance of being simple because it cleverly integrates the constraints.

It comes down to looking for a simple but effective expression to solve a problem. To do this, I seek to integrate complex technologies, new technical possibilities, new manufacturing processes and innovative materials within a harmonious framework for the consumer.

Thus, it is the simplest objects that remain the most difficult to produce from an industrial point of view. Objects that appear to be easy to make are always the result of a long and thoughtful design process. When we look at the “Everywhere” lamp designed for Artemide, the simplicity hides an immense amount of work. It’s precisely because we don’t suspect for a moment that I worked for more than a year to find the fairest solution… And so much the better!
